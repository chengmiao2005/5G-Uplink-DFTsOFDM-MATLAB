EXPERIMENT DESIGN AND DEFINITIONS

Research question
Under matched bandwidth, QAM order, CP overhead, and mean PA-input energy,
how does localized DFT spreading change PAPR, error probability, and
distortion relative to CP-OFDM in a simplified 5G uplink waveform study?

Scope
Single user, SISO, uncoded QPSK and Gray 16-QAM. Ideal synchronization.
Perfect channel knowledge. Localized allocation of 72 contiguous carriers.
Nfft=256, Ncp=18, subcarrier spacing=15 kHz. Waveform experiments use L=4
oversampling (N=1024, CP=72 samples) without changing bandwidth or duration.
The fixed CP is a teaching simplification. No claim of full NR compliance.

System model
d is a K by 1 vector of unit-average-energy QAM symbols, K=72.
F_K and F_N are unitary DFT matrices. P maps K carriers to N FFT bins.
CP-OFDM:       x = F_N^H P d.
DFT-s-OFDM:    x = F_N^H P F_K d.
Each useful block has expected energy K and N samples.
Mean input sample power = K/N. CP multiplies expected block energy by
c=(Nfft+Ncp)/Nfft. The signal energy per information bit is c/log2(M).
Complex noise variance per FFT bin is therefore
N0 = c / (log2(M) * 10^(EbN0dB/10)).
This convention explicitly includes CP energy but excludes coding/pilot cost.

Linear channel
AWGN: H[k]=1.
Rayleigh: four independent circular complex Gaussian taps with average
powers proportional to [1,10^(-3/10),10^(-6/10),10^(-9/10)] at base-rate
delays [0,1,3,5]. Their ensemble average total power is one. Individual
realizations are NOT normalized; doing so would suppress block fading.
Taps remain constant within one block and are independent across blocks.
There is no Doppler/mobility model. CP=18 base-rate samples exceeds delay 5.
After valid CP removal: Y[k]=H[k]X[k]+W[k].
ZF: Z[k]=Y[k]/H[k]. DFT-s-OFDM applies F_K^H after equalization.
The efficient BER loop uses this exactly equivalent frequency-domain model.
The built-in test separately compares it with CP insertion, linear time-domain
convolution, CP removal, and FFT. Deep fades are not clipped or discarded.
No claim is made that ZF is an optimal receiver.

Why AWGN should be a control
Unitary spreading preserves noise statistics and energy. With a linear
AWGN channel, matched QAM and matched Eb/N0, both waveforms should follow
the same theoretical BER within Monte Carlo variability. A large gap is a
reason to debug scaling, mapping, or noise, not automatically a benefit.

Nonlinear experiment
Use a memoryless hard limiter as an illustrative PA model:
g(x)=x min(1,A/|x|), with the zero-input value defined as zero.
IBO=10 log10(A^2/(K/N)). At each IBO, both waveforms have the same threshold
and same ensemble PA-input mean power. No output-power renormalization.
Eb/N0 remains referenced to pre-PA energy. Output/input power ratio is
reported separately, because clipping changes radiated energy.
For each waveform/IBO, a separate 512-block calibration sequence estimates
the scalar gain alpha=Re(sum(conj(x)g(x)))/sum(|x|^2).
The receiver divides by alpha, an idealized gain-estimation assumption.
This is not a measured amplifier, not an efficiency model, and does not
include AM/PM conversion or memory. Do not infer battery-life savings.
The PA BER experiment uses 16-QAM, an AWGN channel, and pre-PA Eb/N0=14 dB.
The EVM experiment removes noise to isolate in-band nonlinear distortion.

Metrics
PAPR = max |x[n]|^2 / mean |x[n]|^2, measured on the useful oversampled block.
CCDF(z) = empirical probability that PAPR in dB exceeds z.
BER = erroneous information bits / tested information bits.
EVM = 100 sqrt(sum |dhat-d|^2 / sum |d|^2), after scalar gain correction.
PSD uses a Hann-window Welch estimator, half overlap, including CP and
block boundaries. All four curves share the linear CP-OFDM peak reference.
Rectangular transmit pulses remain unchanged. DFT spreading is not a filter.

Sampling and uncertainty
Quick: 3000 PAPR blocks and up to 150000 bits per BER point, with at least
256 independent blocks where the bit budget permits. Used only to debug.
Full: 30000 PAPR blocks and up to 2000000 bits per BER point, with at least
2048 blocks. Stop when both waveforms reach 1000 errors after the minimum
block count, or when the common bit budget is exhausted.
Full PA experiment: 8000 blocks per IBO per waveform. Seed=20261027.
Paired comparisons share data, channel realizations, and frequency-domain
noise within each point. New blocks are independent within the stated model.
CSV includes actual bit counts and errors, not only rounded BER.
Rayleigh errors within a block are correlated. Save the empirical standard
error of block BERs rather than an iid-bit confidence interval.
Zero-error points remain exactly zero in raw CSV and are omitted on log plots.
The absence of observed errors is not proof of an error-free link.
PAPR q99 is more stable than q999. Avoid over-interpreting a small tail count.

Interpretation checklist
1. Check both AWGN curves against theoretical QPSK/16-QAM references first.
2. Compare PAPR at the same CCDF and report the tested block count.
3. Interpret multipath BER with the explicitly chosen ZF receiver.
4. Read PA BER together with EVM, gain, and output power loss.
5. Do not claim a bandwidth increase, universal BER superiority, real battery
   savings, coverage gain, or full standards compliance from this experiment.
6. Report all measured outcomes, including null results or disadvantages.
